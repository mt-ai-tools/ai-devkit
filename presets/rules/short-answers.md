# Short answers

An answer is arranged so the reader can stop early. The first line says
what is going on and what is wanted from them; every line after it is
one they could have stopped before. Nothing padded, nothing said twice,
and nothing true left out to keep it short — it goes further down.

- Lead with the conclusion, in everyday words — the first sentence
  answers the question, and a reader who has never seen the project
  follows it. No term used before it is explained.
- Say what the reader would see change; no rule or type-system
  vocabulary.
- Complete sentences. No walls of text.
- Where the answer needs a decision, the question opens it — one
  sentence the reader can answer as it stands, with the options named.
- When work is stopped, the stop is the first line: what is stopped,
  what would unstick it, and the ways forward named — waiting for
  somebody else counts as one. Whoever caused the stop, it still comes
  first.
- Say what happens next before what is already done. The next action
  and what it waits on come first; finished work goes last and short.
- Write so it reads cold. Name the subject by something with exactly one
  meaning — the command as it is typed, the path, the identifier. A step
  is named with the count of all steps and what it does: "step 5 of 9,
  the sign-in form", never "step 5" alone.
- Never point back at what the reader is not holding: not "it", not
  "that approach", not "as said above", and not a numbered question or a
  position in a list that lives somewhere else.
- Say it in words and give the path. Code is the last thing to reach
  for. Where the subject is words — a comment, a document, a message, a
  failing line of output — showing them is showing the subject itself.
  Where the subject is code, one line quoted inside a sentence is the
  most that should be needed; wanting more means the point has not been
  found yet.
- Cut words that carry nothing; never cut a word that carries something.
  Whether a thing is new or already given, finished or still open, is
  information.
- What changes what the operator does next comes first.
- One recommendation, not a survey.

# The closing loop

"Done" means the sweep has come back empty, not that the steps ran.

A step's report that names no next step, and does not say the work is
done, is first asked:

- `whole-brief-done` — instead of the report passing on:
  > Is the whole brief done?

Once the work is said to be done, a round is two looks, the cleanup
first:

1. `cleanup-look` — the cleanup:
   > Should we move, change, delete or update anything since your work
   > is done? Take a look around. Take your time. Don't propose things
   > that belong to other ongoing sessions' work.
2. `use-look` — then putting the new thing to use:
   > Your work introduced something new. Find every place in the frame
   > that should now use it: code still doing the same job by hand,
   > older copies of it, workarounds and comments waiting for it ('until
   > X exists'), and pending lines or briefs it unblocks. Take your
   > time. Don't propose things that belong to other ongoing sessions'
   > work.

Only where it is the same job: the new thing replaces hand-made copies
of itself, never "could also be used for".

Each finding is filtered first, by the agent:

- It belongs to this brief (its summary and what it touches): asked as
  a question, one at a time.
- It belongs elsewhere and is already written down, in another brief or
  the notes: dropped.
- It is written down nowhere and quick (no decision needed, a few lines
  in one module, nobody else's uncommitted edits there): fixed in
  passing — unless another session's brief works where it lies, which
  makes it a hand-off, or its files hold changes nobody committed,
  which parks it.
- A place to use the new thing in an area another session is working
  in: a hand-off written into that session's brief.
- Anything else: a line in the notes, or "write a brief for this?" to
  the operator.

Rounds repeat until both looks come back with nothing that belongs
here. After three rounds that still find something belonging here, the
operator is told, with the list; rounds whose findings all belong
elsewhere do not count. When a round comes back empty, the stand-in
finishes the brief with the organizer's done, the agent runs the
case-writer, the paths done prints and the cases written are committed,
and the end report lists what was built and whether the full check
passed, the decisions settled silently, what was fixed in passing,
dropped and parked, how many test cases were written, skipped and held
back, and what finishing freed.

Each message the stand-in sends is the quote under its name, word for
word.
